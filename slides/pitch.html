01 · THE PROBLEM

A stability number with no error bar is not usable

Peptide–HLA complex half-life decides whether a T cell ever sees an epitope, so it drives neoantigen and vaccine shortlists. Measuring it is slow; models are fast.

But a model that returns 10.3 h and a model that returns 10.3 h, and I have never seen anything like this lead to completely different experiments. Existing predictors only give you the first.

So we did not ask "can we predict half-life". We asked whether the model can flag its own unreliable predictions — and we report where that fails.

Four splits, locked before any modelling: random, peptide-disjoint, cluster (Hamming ≤ 3), and allele-disjoint. The last one is the hard one.

03 · APPROACH

Same network, three ways of describing the allele

PEPTIDE + HLA→FEATURES→5 BOOTSTRAP COPIES→PREDICTION + DISAGREEMENT→CONFORMAL INTERVAL
  • M1 — BLOSUM50 peptide + a one-hot allele label. The label is useless for an allele it never saw.
  • M2 — frozen ESM-2 embeddings of peptide and groove.
  • Pocket — BLOSUM50 of the 34 groove residues that actually contact the peptide. A new allele is a new vector, not an unknown word.
Uncertainty = spread of five bootstrapped copies, converted to a 90% interval by split conformal calibration fitted on held-out alleles. Nothing is tuned on test.

The one-hot model is worse than random on a new allele. Describing the pocket chemistry instead of naming the allele is what makes transfer possible at all.
05 · RESULT 2

The uncertainty is real where the allele is known

On unseen peptides, throwing away the half the copies most disagree about drops mean error from 4.23 h to 3.49 h. Discarding a random half gives 3.90 h, so the ranking is doing work, not the smaller sample.

A claimed 90% interval that covers 89% is calibrated. This is the part most demos skip, and it is the only reason the number on screen means anything.
06 · THE HONEST BIT

On a genuinely new allele, it stops working

Ranking collapses from 0.71 to 0.17. Worse, the ensemble's own disagreement stops tracking the error — it even points the wrong way.

Error–uncertainty correlationUnseen peptideUnseen allele
M1 ensemble0.122−0.096
Pocket ensemble0.167−0.189

Per-allele coverage of the 90% interval runs from 72% to 98% across the ten held-out calibration alleles. The guarantee is marginal, not per-allele.

We could have shown only the peptide split and claimed calibrated uncertainty. The app instead refuses to print an interval for an allele it never saw.
07 · IMPROVING THE WARNING

A learned variance head sharpens it — where it already worked

Predicting the variance directly makes the warning 56% stronger and the interval a third narrower, for a small cost in ranking.

On a new allele it is still flat: error–uncertainty 0.012, coverage 85%. Two independent methods fail the same way, which says the problem is the data, not the head.

08 · THE BASELINE QUESTION

NetMHCstabpan scores 0.86 — on data it was trained on

NetMHCstabpan reaches 0.863 on our peptide split and 0.845 on the held-out allele. Both numbers are in-sample: this exact dataset is its training data.

So we do not claim to beat it, and we do not quietly drop it. We show it on every prediction, labelled as a contaminated ceiling rather than a fair baseline. Being able to say which comparisons are meaningless is part of the result.
09 · THE PRODUCT

Every prediction ships with its own caveat

  • Prediction — half-life in hours, from five bootstrapped copies.
  • Disagreement — how far those copies spread. Not a probability.
  • Calibrated interval — only when the allele was in training. Otherwise withheld.
  • Structure — AlphaFold groove with the 34 contact residues, labelled as a reference protein with no peptide in it. It explains the features; it does not justify the uncertainty.

10 · WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM

And what we would do next

  • No HLA-C result. The dataset has zero HLA-C measurements, so there is no HLA-C accuracy to report and we report none.
  • Disagreement is a proxy for predictive uncertainty, not a probability and not biology.
  • pLDDT is AlphaFold's confidence in coordinates, not our model's confidence.

Next: conditional conformal per allele group, so the 90% holds per allele rather than on average; and measured half-lives for a handful of unseen alleles, which is the only thing that turns the new-allele failure into a solved problem.
